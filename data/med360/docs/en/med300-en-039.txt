Your results after surgery

As you asked, I am writing to explain your results in plain language. A copy goes to your family doctor in Valletta, Dr Marthese Vancell.

What the surgery found
The part of the bowel removed at your right hemicolectomy in September contained a stage II colon cancer (pT3 N0). The cancer had grown into the bowel wall but none of the 24 lymph nodes removed contained cancer (0 of 24), and the margins were clear, meaning the surgeon removed it completely.

Your scan
Your CT scan of the chest, abdomen and pelvis showed no evidence of spread to the liver, lungs or elsewhere.

The special tests
Tests on the tumour showed loss of the MLH1 protein (MMR-deficient). Because of this we tested your blood for an inherited cause. This showed an MLH1 pathogenic variant, which means you have Lynch syndrome. Lynch syndrome raises the risk of bowel cancer and some other cancers, which is why regular checks matter.

What this means for treatment
You do not need chemotherapy. For stage II cancers with this tumour type, chemotherapy does not add benefit. Instead we will keep a close watch:
- Colonoscopy in 1 year (November 2027), then every 2 years because of Lynch syndrome.
- CEA blood test every 6 months - Dr Vancell can take this in Malta and send us the result.

Your family
Lynch syndrome can run in families. Your sister Nuala Seoighe and other blood relatives can be offered a test for the same change. I have referred you to the clinical genetics service, who will write to you and can prepare a letter for your family.
